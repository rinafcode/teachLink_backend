This policy states who may speak to the media on the project's behalf, the
approval process for doing so, and the messaging guidance that applies. It
governs project-facing media contact, not an individual commenting on their own
work.

## 1. Who speaks to media

- **Official spokespeople are designated maintainers.** The maintainer team keeps
  a short list of people authorised to speak to the press for the project. Only
  those people may give an on-the-record interview or a statement attributed to
  the project.
- **Anyone may be approached.** If a journalist contacts you, you are welcome to
  acknowledge the message, but you must not give a project statement unless you
  are a designated spokesperson.
- **On background, not on the record.** A non-designated contributor may talk
  privately for context, clearly flagged as off the record and not attributable
  to TeachLink, and must pass any request for an official comment to a
  spokesperson.
- **Personal views stay personal.** If you speak publicly about your own work,
  say you are speaking for yourself unless you are a designated spokesperson —
  see [`community/REPRESENTATION.md`](REPRESENTATION.md).

## 2. Approval process

1. **Forward the request.** Send the journalist's request, the outlet, and the
   proposed questions to the maintainer team through a private channel.
2. **Assign a spokesperson.** A maintainer with relevant knowledge takes the
   request, or declines it.
3. **Prepare the talking points.** Before the interview, the spokesperson drafts
   the key messages and gets agreement from at least one other maintainer.
   Security and legal topics are cleared through the channels in §4.
4. **Coordinate timing.** Public-facing announcements are timed so the
   repository, release notes, or an advisory is the source of record; media
   coverage links to it, not the other way round.
5. **Pre-notice for sensitive topics.** Anything touching security, legal
   matters, funding, or a partner must be reviewed under
   [`SECURITY_POLICY.md`](../SECURITY_POLICY.md) and, for legal matters, with
   counsel before any statement is given.
6. **Post-interview record.** The spokesperson files the final quotes or a link
   to the published article with the maintainer team.

## 3. Messaging guidance

- **Be factual and specific.** Prefer what shipped, when, and what it does over
  adjectives. If a number is used, cite the source.
- **Stay on your area.** Answer what you know; defer questions outside your area
  to a maintainer who knows it.
- **Never speculate on the roadmap.** Future features are described as "under
  discussion" unless a public issue or roadmap document states otherwise.
- **Say "I don't know" freely.** A wrong answer costs more than a deferred one.
- **Match the tone in [`policies/COMMUNICATION_NORMS.md`](../policies/COMMUNICATION_NORMS.md)**:
  respectful, no snark, no criticism of other projects or individuals.
- **Credit contributors.** Name the people who did the work when it is public.

## 4. Security, privacy, and legal topics

- **Do not discuss unpatched vulnerabilities.** Refer any security question to
  the private process in [`SECURITY_POLICY.md`](../SECURITY_POLICY.md) and may
  only comment after an advisory is published.
- **Do not disclose personal data** about users, contributors, or partners.
- **Legal questions** (licensing, trademarks, disputes) are answered only after
  review by the maintainers and, where needed, counsel, per
  [`policies/LICENSING.md`](../policies/LICENSING.md).
- **Sanctions and financial topics** follow
  [`compliance/SANCTIONS.md`](../compliance/SANCTIONS.md).

## 5. If something goes wrong

- If a spokesperson gives an inaccurate statement, correct it promptly through
  the same outlet and note the correction with the maintainer team.
- If an unauthorised person speaks for the project, a maintainer issues a short,
  factual clarification and reminds the community of this policy without
  personal attacks.
- Repeated misrepresentation is a conduct matter under
  [`CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md`](../CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md).
