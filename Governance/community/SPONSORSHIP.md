This policy defines the forms of sponsorship the project accepts, the
conflict-of-interest rules that apply, and the transparency commitments the
project keeps. It exists so that funding never buys influence over the project's
technical direction or its Code of Conduct.

## 1. Accepted sponsorship forms

The project may accept:

- **Unrestricted financial donations** to the community treasury, with no
  conditions on how the funds are used.
- **Directed grants** for a named, agreed purpose (for example, documentation or
  accessibility work), where the purpose is public and does not create control
  over roadmap or staffing.
- **In-kind contributions** — infrastructure, CI minutes, tooling, or event
  space — that are disclosed and that the project can decline or stop using at
  any time.
- **Event and program sponsorship** that follows
  [`community/EVENTS_POLICY.md`](EVENTS_POLICY.md).

The project does **not** accept:

- funding conditioned on a change to the Code of Conduct, moderation, or review
  outcomes;
- sponsorship from a party under applicable sanctions (see
  [`compliance/SANCTIONS.md`](../compliance/SANCTIONS.md));
- anonymous funding above the threshold in §3 that cannot be screened;
- any arrangement that would make the project an instrument of a sponsor's
  marketing or legal dispute.

## 2. Conflict-of-interest rules

- **Disclose before discussion.** Anyone taking part in a decision about a
  sponsor must disclose any financial or personal interest in that sponsor.
- **No self-approval.** A person with a conflict may provide information but must
  not cast the deciding vote or approve the arrangement alone; a second,
  unconflicted maintainer approves.
- **No sponsor in the driver's seat.** Sponsors do not sit on the technical
  decision path, do not get a veto over releases, and do not review moderation.
- **No preferential treatment.** A sponsor's bug report, PR, or feature request
  is triaged like any other; money does not change priority.
- **Cooling-off.** A maintainer who takes a paid role with a sponsor discloses it
  and recuses from sponsorship decisions involving that sponsor.

## 3. Transparency commitments

- **A public register.** Accepted sponsors, the form and amount (or "in-kind"),
  and any stated purpose are listed in the project's public sponsorship register
  or transparency report, using the cadence in
  [`policies/TRANSPARENCY_REPORTS.md`](../policies/TRANSPARENCY_REPORTS.md).
- **No undisclosed funding.** Funding above a small, published threshold is
  always disclosed; only genuinely trivial donations may be aggregated rather
  than itemised, and the aggregation rule is published.
- **Treasury reporting.** The treasurer records income and spending and reports
  it per [`roles/TREASURER.md`](../roles/TREASURER.md).
- **Screening.** Before accepting, the responsible maintainer or treasurer
  screens the party as required by
  [`compliance/SANCTIONS.md`](../compliance/SANCTIONS.md) and records the check.
- **Withdrawal.** The project may decline or return a sponsorship at any time,
  including if the sponsor's conduct conflicts with
  [`CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md`](../CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md).

## 4. Process

1. Open an issue describing the offer, the form, the amount, and any conditions.
2. The treasurer and a maintainer without a conflict review it against §1–§3.
3. Record the decision and the screening outcome; update the public register.
4. Announce accepted sponsorship factually and link the register entry.
