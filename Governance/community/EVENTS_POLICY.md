This policy describes how community events are proposed, approved, run, and
sponsored. It applies to online and in-person events, whether hosted by a
maintainer, a contributor, or an external partner using the project's name.

## 1. What counts as a project event

An event is "in the project's name" if it is advertised as a TeachLink event,
uses the project's name or marks, is funded by the project or its treasury, or is
run by a maintainer acting in their project role. A private gathering among
contributors, without project branding or funds, is not a project event and does
not need approval.

## 2. Proposing an event

1. **Open an issue** using the community label and describe:
   - the event's purpose and audience;
   - the format, date, time zone, and expected size;
   - the organiser and any co-organisers;
   - the budget and where the funds would come from;
   - any sponsors and what they receive in return.
2. **Include a rough run-of-show** (duration, sessions, and who leads each) and
   the channel where attendees will be asked to follow the Code of Conduct.
3. **Allow time.** Proposals should be opened at least **14 days** before an
   online event and **30 days** before an in-person event so maintainers can
   review budget and logistics without rushing.
4. **Name a single accountable organiser.** That person is the contact for
   conduct issues during the event.

## 3. Approval

- A maintainer (see [`roles/MAINTAINER.md`](../roles/MAINTAINER.md)) approves the
  event and records the decision on the issue.
- Events that spend treasury funds or sign a sponsorship also follow
  [`community/SPONSORSHIP.md`](SPONSORSHIP.md) and the treasurer's review.
- If the organiser is also the approving maintainer, a second maintainer must
  approve, so no one approves their own spend.
- Approval is for a specific event; recurring events are re-approved at least
  once per quarter.

## 4. Code of Conduct applicability

- The [`CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md`](../CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md) applies to every project
  event, online and in person, including side chats and socials.
- The event page, invitations, and any pre-event communication must link the Code
  of Conduct and state how to reach the organiser privately.
- The organiser (or a named delegate) must be reachable for the whole event and
  must be able to act on a report, including removing a participant.
- For in-person events, publish what the escalation path will be on the day; for
  online events, state which private channel a report may use. See
  [`processes/ESCALATION_PATH.md`](../processes/ESCALATION_PATH.md).
- A report is handled under [`CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md`](../CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md) and
  [`policies/MODERATION.md`](../policies/MODERATION.md); the organiser does not
  decide the outcome alone.

## 5. Sponsorship and funds

- Any sponsor, in-kind or cash, must be disclosed on the event issue before the
  event is announced.
- Sponsored events must not give a sponsor editorial control over talks,
  speakers, or the Code of Conduct.
- Sponsors that conflict with the project's values or that are under sanctions
  screening (see [`compliance/SANCTIONS.md`](../compliance/SANCTIONS.md)) are not
  accepted.
- A post-event note is posted on the issue: attendance, a short summary, and how
  funds were spent. Transparency is the default, not an extra.

## 6. After the event

- The organiser posts a short debrief on the issue within **7 days**, including
  any conduct reports (without personal detail) and lessons learned.
- Recorded material, if published, follows
  [`community/MEDIA_POLICY.md`](MEDIA_POLICY.md).
- Funded events that do not happen are cancelled on the issue and any committed
  funds are returned to the treasury.
