This policy states who may represent the project, how approval works, and the
disclosure rules that apply. Its purpose is simple: a listener or reader should
always know whether they are hearing from the project or from an individual.

## 1. Who may represent the project

- **The maintainer team represents the project.** A maintainer may speak for the
  project on their area of responsibility by default.
- **Project representation is a role, not a title.** A contributor may represent
  the project at a specific event or in a specific forum if a maintainer
  authorises it for that occasion (see §2). That authorisation is scoped and
  time-limited.
- **The lead maintainer is the final voice** on matters of project position that
  are contested or that touch legal, security, or treasury questions. See
  [`processes/RELEASE_SIGNOFF.md`](../processes/RELEASE_SIGNOFF.md) for the
  analogous release authority.
- **No one else speaks for the project.** A contributor without authorisation
  may speak about their own work but not as TeachLink.

## 2. Approval process

1. **Ask first.** Before accepting an invitation to represent the project, send
   the event or outlet, the audience, the topic, and any recording plan to the
   maintainer team.
2. **Scope the authorisation.** A maintainer confirms or declines and records the
   scope: which topics, which date, and whether the talk is on the record.
3. **Prepare the material.** Slides and talking points are shared with a
   maintainer before the event. Anything on security, legal, or funding topics is
   cleared under [`community/MEDIA_POLICY.md`](MEDIA_POLICY.md) §2.
4. **Use the project marks correctly.** Branding follows
   [`BRAND_USAGE.md`](../BRAND_USAGE.md).
5. **Report back.** After the event, the speaker files a short note with a link,
   so the community can find the talk.

## 3. Disclosure rules

- **State your capacity.** Begin by saying whether you are speaking for the
  project or for yourself. When in doubt, say "I speak for myself".
- **Disclose affiliation and funding.** If an employer, sponsor, or client is
  relevant to your talk, say so. Travel or fees paid by a third party are
  disclosed.
- **Do not imply endorsement.** Do not present a vendor tool, a partner, or a
  sponsor as "officially recommended" unless the project has decided it publicly.
- **Be accurate about status.** Describe unreleased work as under discussion, not
  as shipped, and cite the public issue or roadmap when you do.
- **Attribute the work.** Credit contributors by name where they are public.
- **Do not disclose private detail.** No secrets, no user data, no unpublished
  vulnerability detail, no private governance discussion.

## 4. Partners and third parties

- A partner may describe their integration with the project factually, but may
  not claim to represent the project or speak to its roadmap.
- A statement that appears to commit the project to a partner must be cleared by
  the maintainers in advance.
- Misrepresentation by a third party is addressed with a short public
  clarification and, if repeated, under
  [`CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md`](../CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md).
