The project depends on people speaking up. This policy explains how to report
suspected wrongdoing, the protection a good-faith reporter receives, how reports
are handled, and how the project avoids retaliation.

## 1. What can be reported

- A breach of law or regulation in the project's activity.
- A serious breach of the [`CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md`](../CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md) or of a
  project policy.
- Financial impropriety, misuse of funds, or a conflict of interest not
  disclosed.
- A security, privacy, or safety risk that is being concealed or ignored.
- Abuse of a maintainer or contributor position, or retaliation against someone
  who raised a concern.

Genuine mistakes and disagreements over technical decisions are **not**
whistleblowing; those follow normal review and
[`processes/CONFLICT_RESOLUTION.md`](../processes/CONFLICT_RESOLUTION.md).

## 2. How to report

- **Preferred channel:** a private written report to the maintainers via the
  confidential address published in
  [`policies/COMMUNICATION_NORMS.md`](../policies/COMMUNICATION_NORMS.md), marked
  "confidential."
- **Security matters:** if the concern involves a vulnerability or an active
  breach, use [`SECURITY_POLICY.md`](../SECURITY_POLICY.md) instead, so the
  response team can contain it.
- **If the concern involves a maintainer**, send the report to a maintainer who
  is not implicated; the recipients of the confidential channel are listed there.
- **Include what you can:** what happened, when, who was involved, and any
  evidence. A report is still worth making if some detail is missing; do not
  delay to gather more.
- **Anonymous reports** are accepted and considered, though a named reporter
  makes follow-up and protection easier.

## 3. Protection for the reporter

- **Good faith is the standard.** A report made honestly, even if it turns out to
  be mistaken, is protected. A knowingly false report made to harm someone is
  not.
- **Confidentiality.** The reporter's identity is kept confidential and shared
  only with those who must know to handle the report, or where the law requires
  it.
- **No retaliation.** No one may be demoted, excluded, discouraged, or otherwise
  penalised for raising a concern or for taking part in an investigation.
  Retaliation is itself a serious breach of this policy.
- **Open-source contributors** cannot be "dismissed," but the equivalent
  protections apply: a good-faith reporter is not blacklisted from the project or
  from review.

## 4. How reports are handled

1. **Acknowledge** receipt within **5 business days** where the reporter gave a
   contact.
2. **Assess and route.** A maintainer with no conflict of interest reviews the
   report and decides the path: a policy process, a security response, a conduct
   process, or a referral to an external authority where appropriate.
3. **Investigate** fairly, giving the person concerned an opportunity to respond,
   and keeping records confidential.
4. **Decide and act** under the applicable policy — for conduct matters,
   [`CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md`](../CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md); for policies,
   [`compliance/SANCTIONS.md`](SANCTIONS.md).
5. **Report the outcome** to the reporter to the extent confidentiality allows,
   without disclosing the identities of others.
6. **Escalate where needed.** If no unconflicted maintainer exists, the report is
   routed to an external advisor; see
   [`processes/ESCALATION_PATH.md`](../processes/ESCALATION_PATH.md).

## 5. Records and review

- Reports, evidence, and outcomes are kept in a restricted register, retained
  only as long as needed for accountability and legal obligation, then deleted.
- The maintainers review this policy at least **every 12 months**, and confirm
  that the confidential channel is staffed and reaches an unconflicted recipient.
