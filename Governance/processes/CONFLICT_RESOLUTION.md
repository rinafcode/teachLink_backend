## Purpose

This process provides a fair, transparent, and consistent way to resolve
disagreements before they harm collaboration, delay project work, or undermine
trust. It gives contributors and maintainers a clear path to raise concerns,
be heard, and reach a proportionate resolution while preserving the project's
respectful, constructive, and professional standards.

## Scope

This process covers interpersonal or working disagreements involving project
participants, including disagreements about contribution or review practices,
project-process expectations, communication, collaboration, and maintainer or
working-group decisions. It may also be used when a disagreement has moved
beyond ordinary discussion on an issue, pull request, or governance thread.

It does not replace the normal pull-request review flow, the objection-handling
process for a proposed governance decision, security reporting, or urgent
incident response. Use the applicable existing process first where it governs
the matter; this process provides support when the disagreement itself remains
unresolved. Conduct concerns may require a private maintainer response under
[`COMMUNICATION_NORMS.md`](../policies/COMMUNICATION_NORMS.md).

## Guiding Principles

- **Respectful communication.** Discuss conduct, work, and evidence without
  personal attacks, dismissiveness, or discriminatory language.
- **Good-faith participation.** Participants should seek understanding and a
  workable resolution, rather than assume improper intent.
- **Transparency.** Project-related reasoning and outcomes should be visible
  in the appropriate repository record unless confidentiality is needed.
- **Neutrality.** Facilitation and decisions must be free from actual or
  perceived conflicts of interest where possible.
- **Project and community interests.** Resolutions should advance the health,
  safety, and sustainable operation of the project and its community.
- **Protection from harm.** No participant may be retaliated against for
  raising a concern in good faith or participating in this process.

## Resolution Steps

1. **Direct discussion.** The involved parties should first discuss the
   disagreement respectfully in the relevant issue, pull request, or other
   appropriate project forum. They should state their concerns, evidence, and
   the outcome they seek. A participant may skip this step when direct contact
   would be unsafe, inappropriate, or unlikely to be productive.
2. **Written clarification.** If direct discussion does not resolve the matter,
   either party documents a concise, factual summary: the issue, relevant
   context, points of agreement and disagreement, and proposed resolution.
   Use the existing public record where suitable; use a private maintainer
   route for sensitive information.
3. **Neutral facilitation.** Either party may ask an uninvolved maintainer or
   other suitable neutral project participant to facilitate. The facilitator
   helps the parties clarify facts, communicate constructively, and identify
   options; they do not decide the outcome at this stage.
4. **Maintainer or community review.** If facilitation does not resolve the
   matter, it moves to maintainers for review. Where the matter falls within a
   working group's authority, maintainers may seek that group's input. A
   disagreement about a proposal or governance decision follows
   [`OBJECTION_HANDLING.md`](OBJECTION_HANDLING.md) and, when required, the
   formal voting process.
5. **Final decision.** If maintainers cannot reach consensus, the Lead
   Maintainer uses the deciding role described in the
   [`CHARTER.md`](../CHARTER.md). The final decision should state its basis,
   any actions, and how it will be recorded or communicated.

## Neutral Facilitator

A neutral facilitator is an uninvolved person trusted to help the parties have
a productive conversation. The facilitator:

- keeps discussion focused on the stated disagreement, evidence, and possible
  resolution;
- gives each party a reasonable opportunity to be heard and checks that their
  views are understood accurately;
- sets respectful communication expectations and may pause or redirect an
  unproductive discussion; and
- identifies common ground, clarifying questions, and next steps without
  favoring a party or preferred result.

The facilitator must remain impartial. They must not investigate privately,
make findings of misconduct, impose a decision, advocate for either party, or
share sensitive information beyond those who need it to resolve the matter.

Before accepting the role, a facilitator must disclose any conflict of interest
that could reasonably call their neutrality into question. A facilitator with a
material conflict, including direct involvement in the disagreement, must step
aside. The parties may request another facilitator; if none is available, the
matter proceeds directly to maintainer review with conflicted decision-makers
recused where practical.

## Escalation Path

Either involved party may move the matter to the next step when a reasonable,
good-faith attempt at the current step has not resolved it, when the other
party does not engage, or when the current step is unsafe or inappropriate.
The written clarification accompanies each escalation so reviewers do not have
to reconstruct the disagreement from scattered discussion.

Maintainer review is the normal final review level. A matter requiring a
binding governance decision follows the established objection-handling and
formal-voting processes. If maintainers cannot settle a matter within their
authority, it is escalated to the Lead Maintainer under the charter. Participants
must not use escalation merely to relitigate a resolved matter without material
new information.

## Documentation and Confidentiality

Document the concern, relevant context and evidence, steps taken, participants
in any facilitation or review, decisions, reasoning, and follow-up actions.
For project-process or technical disagreements, the default is a public issue,
pull request, or governance record, consistent with the communication norms.

Keep documentation private when public disclosure could expose personal,
sensitive, security-related, or otherwise confidential information, or could
create a safety risk. Share sensitive information only with people who need it
to resolve the matter, and do not include secrets, private contact details, or
unnecessary personal information in the record.

When a decision affects project policy, governance, access, or future
contributors, maintainers should record an appropriately redacted outcome and
reasoning publicly. Significant decisions are recorded in
[`DECISION_LOG.md`](../DECISION_LOG.md) in accordance with that document; the
public record must not reveal confidential details.

## Outcomes

Possible outcomes include:

- an agreement between the parties, with any commitments recorded;
- clarification of project expectations, responsibilities, or communication
  practices;
- a maintainer decision, including a decision to uphold or change an existing
  project action;
- a proposal to clarify or update a process or policy through the normal
  governance process; or
- other proportionate project actions consistent with existing governance and
  communication norms.

## Retaliation and Bad-Faith Behavior

Retaliation, harassment, intimidation, or deliberate misuse of this process is
not acceptable. This includes punishing a person for raising a concern in good
faith, attempting to silence participation, knowingly making false claims, or
using the process to harass another participant. Such behavior may be handled
by maintainers under the applicable project policies.

## Review and Maintenance

Maintainers own this process and may review and update it as the project
evolves, particularly when related communication, decision-making, or conduct
processes change. Amendments follow the normal governance process in
[`README.md`](../README.md) and take effect when merged.
